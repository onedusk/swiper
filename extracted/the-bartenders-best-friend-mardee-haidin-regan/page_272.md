Red Snapper
In 1934, when Vincent Astor hired Fernand “Pete” Petiot
as a bartender at New York’s St. Regis Hotel, Astor
wasn’t keen on the name of Petiot’s signature drink,
The Bloody Mary, and changed it to The Red Snapper.
The original name remained its popular moniker,
though.

cubes. Shake well. Strain into an ice-filled Highball glass.

Red Wine Cooler
4 ounces dry red wine
4 ounces lemon-lime soda
Lemon twist, for garnish

Pour the wine and soda into an ice-filled Collins glass. Stir
briefly; add the twist.

Redheaded Whore
1 1/2 ounces brandy
1 1/2 ounces sloe gin
1/2 ounce peach schnapps

Pour all of the ingredients into a mixing glass two-thirds full of
ice cubes. Stir well. Strain into a chilled Cocktail glass or an icefilled Rocks glass.

Remsen Cooler
The original cooler, this drink is often erroneously made
with gin. Remsen used to be a brand name of scotch—
the true base of this drink.
