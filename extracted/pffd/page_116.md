Choosing between Chapter 7 and 13
You can file one of two forms of personal bankruptcy: Chapter 7 or Chapter
13. Chapter 7 allows you to discharge or cancel certain debts. This form
of bankruptcy makes the most sense when you have significant debts that
you’re legally allowed to cancel. (See “Understanding bankruptcy benefits,”
earlier in this chapter, for details on which debts can be canceled, or
discharged.)
Chapter 13 comes up with a repayment schedule that requires you to pay
your debts over several years. Chapter 13 stays on your credit record (just
like Chapter 7), but it doesn’t eliminate debt, so its value is limited — usually
to dealing with debts like taxes that can’t be discharged through bankruptcy.
Chapter 13 can keep creditors at bay until you work out a repayment
schedule in the courts.
